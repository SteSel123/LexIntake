# LexIntake FAQs

Common questions used during prospective-client intake. Answers are general guidance for agents—not legal advice to the public.

## Getting started

**How long does intake take?**  
Usually 10–20 minutes. Complex medical malpractice, employment, or immigration matters may need a follow-up call.

**What should I prepare before intake?**  
Dates and locations, names of people/companies involved, injuries or harm, insurance or claim numbers, court dates if any, and copies of key documents (photos, contracts, medical bills, charge papers, USCIS notices).

**Can someone else complete intake for me?**  
Yes, a family member or interpreter may help. The firm must still confirm identity and authority before representation.

## Case screening

**Who decides if my case is accepted?**  
An attorney reviews the intake against practice-area acceptance criteria, conflicts, SOL, damages, and attorney availability.

**Why might a case be declined?**  
Common reasons: expired statute of limitations, conflict of interest, no viable legal theory, damages below firm minimums, or no attorney available in that jurisdiction/practice area.

**What if I’m unsure which practice area applies?**  
Describe what happened in plain language. The intake agent will map your facts to the closest practice area(s) and may route for dual screening (e.g., workplace injury → Workers’ Compensation and/or Employment Law).

## Deadlines (statutes of limitations)

**What is a statute of limitations?**  
A legal deadline to file a claim or charge presentation. Missing it can permanently bar the case. Deadlines differ by practice area and state—see `sol_tables.json`.

**Should I wait until I have all records?**  
No. Submit intake promptly so deadlines and conflicts can be checked. Records can follow.

**Do public-entity claims have shorter deadlines?**  
Often yes (e.g., California Tort Claims Act presentment, municipal notice-of-claim rules). Tell the agent if a city, county, state, or police agency is involved.

## Fees and costs

**Is the consultation free?**  
Personal Injury, Workers’ Compensation, many Product Liability / Medical Malpractice, and many Civil Rights / Consumer fee-shifting matters are typically free to evaluate. Family Law, Immigration, and Criminal Defense may require a consultation fee or retainer discussion.

**What is a contingency fee?**  
You pay attorney fees only if there is a recovery. Common in Personal Injury, Medical Malpractice, Product Liability, and many Workers’ Comp matters (subject to statutory approval/caps).

**What is a retainer?**  
An advance deposit billed against hourly work, common in Family Law, Employment (hourly matters), Immigration, and Criminal Defense.

**Are court or filing costs included in fees?**  
Usually separate. Contingency practices often advance costs and reimburse from recovery; hourly/flat-fee matters usually require clients to fund costs.

## Practice-area specifics

**Personal Injury — Do I need a police report?**  
Helpful but not always required. Photos, medical visit proof, and insurance info can still support intake.

**Employment Law — Do I need to file with EEOC/CRD first?**  
Many discrimination claims require an administrative charge before court. Intake will ask what you already filed and when.

**Immigration — Can you guarantee approval?**  
No. Outcomes depend on facts, evidence, and agency/court decisions. Fees are not contingency-based.

**Family Law — Can fees be contingency?**  
Generally no for divorce/custody. Expect hourly billing against a retainer.

**Criminal Defense — Will talking to intake create attorney-client privilege?**  
Treat intake as confidential firm communication, but representation begins only when an engagement agreement is signed (or the firm otherwise formally agrees to represent you).

**Workers’ Compensation — Do I pay upfront?**  
Usually no. Fees are typically a percentage approved under state rules and paid from benefits/recovery.

**Medical Malpractice — Why do you need records so quickly?**  
Experts must review whether care fell below the standard of care. Without records, the firm cannot responsibly accept the case.

**Product Liability — Should I keep the product?**  
Yes, if safe to do so. Preserve the product, packaging, manuals, and receipts; do not throw them away.

**Civil Rights — What if bodycam video exists?**  
Note agency, date, officers’ names/badge numbers if known. The firm may send preservation letters and public-records requests.

**Consumer Protection — Is a small dollar loss worth pursuing?**  
Sometimes, especially under fee-shifting statutes (FDCPA, FCRA, CLRA). Intake will assess damages, statutory penalties, and fee recovery.

## After intake

**How soon will I hear back?**  
Urgent criminal custody, imminent removal hearings, and approaching SOL matters are prioritized—often same business day. Other matters typically within 1–2 business days.

**What if my case is declined?**  
You should receive a clear decline (and, when appropriate, referral suggestions). Keep your own deadline calendar regardless.

**When does representation start?**  
Only after conflicts clearance and a signed engagement agreement (or other formal acceptance by the firm).
